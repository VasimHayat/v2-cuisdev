## Good slugs in one step

A slug turns a page title into the part of the address people see, share and type. Most CMSs generate one automatically, but the result is often too long, full of stop words, or broken by accents and symbols: `10-tips-%26-tricks` instead of `10-tips-and-tricks`. This generator gives you control over every rule and handles a whole list of titles at once, which is useful when migrating a site, planning a content calendar or naming files.

Each title is cleaned in the same order: replacement rules first, so `&` becomes `and`, then transliteration, then splitting into words on anything that is not a letter or digit. Stop words and pure numbers are removed if you ask, the words are lower-cased and joined with your separator, and the result is trimmed to the maximum length at a word boundary.

## Transliteration and international titles

Accented Latin letters are stripped to their base letter with Unicode decomposition, and letters that do not decompose, such as ß, æ, ø and ł, use explicit mappings. Cyrillic and Greek follow common romanisation tables, so a Russian or Greek title still produces a readable ASCII slug. If your site supports Unicode URLs and you prefer to keep the original script, turn transliteration off.
