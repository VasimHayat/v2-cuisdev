## One tool for images to PDF and merging

Most PDF sites split this into separate tools and upload your files to do it. This converter keeps both jobs together and keeps the files on your device. Add whatever you have, in whatever mix, arrange the pages, and create a single PDF. A stack of phone photos of receipts becomes an expense report; three quarterly PDFs become one annual document; a JPG cover page goes in front of a generated report.

The order of the list is the order of the pages. Drag rows to rearrange them, or use Sort when your files are already numbered in their names. The rotate button on an image fixes a sideways photo before it goes in.

## How image pages are built

With Page size set to Fit to image, each page takes the image's own proportions at 96 pixels per inch, so a 1920 by 1080 screenshot becomes a 20 by 11.25 inch page with no borders. That is the right choice for screenshots and photos that will be viewed on screen. For printing, choose a paper size: the image is centred inside the margins, either scaled to fit entirely or scaled to fill the page and cropped at the edges, and Auto orientation turns the page sideways for landscape pictures.

JPEG and PNG files are embedded exactly as they are, which keeps the PDF small and the quality untouched. Formats that PDF cannot hold directly, such as WebP, AVIF, GIF and BMP, are converted on a canvas first. Images with transparency are saved as PNG so the background stays clear; everything else becomes JPEG at the quality you choose.

## Merging PDFs with page ranges

Each PDF in the list shows its page count and an optional range box. Leave it empty to include every page, or type ranges in the same style as a print dialog: `1-3, 7` picks four pages, `10-` runs to the end, and `-2` takes the first two. Pages are copied with their original size, rotation and content, including vector graphics and text, so the merged file stays searchable.
