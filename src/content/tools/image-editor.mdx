## A photo editor for the quick jobs

Most image edits are small: trim the edges of a screenshot, straighten a photo that came out sideways, bring a header down to the size a platform wants, or brighten a picture before it goes into a document. Opening a desktop application for those jobs is slow, and uploading the image to a website is not something you want to do with a photo of your passport. This editor does the small jobs in your browser, on a canvas, with nothing sent anywhere.

Every edit is kept as a description of what to do to the original image rather than as a changed copy. That is why undo and redo are instant, why rotating after cropping keeps the crop in the right place, and why the exported file is rendered from the original pixels in one pass instead of degrading through several saves.

## Crop, then resize

The crop box selects the area that matters; the resize sets how large that area is exported. Keeping the two separate avoids the most common mistake, which is stretching an image into a size with a different shape. Choosing a size preset therefore crops to the preset's aspect ratio first, centred on the image, and then sets the output size. Drag the crop box to choose which part of the picture survives.

When you resize with the aspect ratio locked, typing a width fills in the matching height and vice versa. Downscaling by a large factor happens in halving steps so that fine detail is averaged properly instead of being dropped, which is what keeps text in screenshots readable at half size.

## Adjustments and compare

The adjustment sliders map to the standard canvas filters: brightness, contrast and saturation as percentages, hue rotation in degrees, grayscale and sepia as a mix, and a blur radius in pixels. They apply to the exported image exactly as they appear in the preview. Hold the Compare button to see the image without them, and double-click any slider to return it to neutral.
