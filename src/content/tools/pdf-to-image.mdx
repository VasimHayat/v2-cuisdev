    a: "Use 72 or 96 DPI for images shown on screen, such as a page preview on a website or a slide in a chat. Use 150 DPI for documents people will zoom into, and 200 or 300 DPI when the image will be printed or the text must stay sharp at full size. Higher DPI means larger files and longer rendering."
  - q: PNG, JPG or WebP?
    a: "PNG is lossless and keeps text and line art perfectly crisp, but it is the largest. JPG is much smaller for pages that are mostly photographs, at the cost of slight blur around text. WebP gives JPG-like quality at a smaller size and is supported by every current browser, but some older desktop software cannot open it."

## Turning PDF pages into pictures

A PDF is the right format for documents and the wrong one for almost everything else. Slides need to go into a chat, a page needs to appear on a website, a diagram needs to go into a presentation, or a form needs to be attached somewhere that only accepts images. This tool renders each page exactly as a PDF viewer would and saves it as a regular image file.

Rendering uses pdf.js, the same engine Firefox uses to display PDFs, so fonts, vector graphics, transparency and embedded images come out as they look in a viewer. Each page is rendered at the resolution you choose rather than captured from the screen, which is why text stays sharp at 200 or 300 DPI.

## Resolution and file size

PDF pages are measured in points, 72 to the inch, so the DPI you pick decides the pixel size directly: an A4 page is 595 by 842 pixels at 72 DPI and 2480 by 3508 pixels at 300 DPI. The pixel size of the first selected page updates as you change the setting, and the tool refuses sizes that would exhaust the browser's memory instead of crashing halfway through a long document.
