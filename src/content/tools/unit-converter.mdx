  - q: Why is 1 GB not 1024 MB here?
    a: "Gigabyte (GB) is an SI unit and means 1,000,000,000 bytes, which is how drive makers and network speeds count. The binary units are gibibyte (GiB), mebibyte (MiB) and so on, where each step is 1024. Operating systems often show GiB but label it GB, which is why a 1 TB drive appears as about 931 GB.   - q: Why does converting fuel economy give such different numbers?

## One converter for everyday units

Recipes in cups, tyre pressures in psi, a weather forecast in Fahrenheit, a download quoted in megabits: unit conversion is a small task that comes up constantly. This converter covers fourteen categories in one place, accepts the way people actually write measurements, and shows the answer together with the rule behind it, so you can check the result or do it in your head next time.

Type the value the way you would say it. A height of 5 ft 11 in, a running time of 1 h 30 min, or a European-style 1,5 all work. The line under the input shows exactly how it was understood.

## Precision and very large or small values

Results are calculated with full double precision. The Precision menu only changes how many significant digits are shown, from three for a quick estimate to fifteen for engineering work. Values beyond a trillion or below a millionth switch to scientific notation, such as 9.46 × 10<sup>15</sup> metres in a light year, and the copy buttons copy the plain number so it pastes cleanly into a spreadsheet.
