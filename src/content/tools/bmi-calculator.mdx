  - q: How is BMI calculated?
    a: "BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in metres. Someone 1.75 m tall weighing 72 kg has a BMI of 72 ÷ 1.75² = 23.5. In imperial units the same result comes from 703 × pounds ÷ inches²; this calculator converts to metric first."
  - q: What are the BMI categories?
    a: "For adults the World Health Organization uses underweight below 18.5, healthy weight 18.5 to 24.9, overweight 25 to 29.9, and obesity from 30, split into class I (30 to 34.9), class II (35 to 39.9) and class III (40 and above). Some health services use lower thresholds for people of South Asian, Chinese and Black African or Caribbean background, often 23 and 27.5."
  - q: Is BMI accurate?
    a: "BMI is a quick screening measure for groups, not a diagnosis for one person. It does not distinguish muscle from fat or say where fat is stored. Muscular people can score as overweight while being lean, and older adults can score as healthy while carrying more fat. Waist measurement and a conversation with a clinician add the context BMI lacks."

## What BMI tells you

Body mass index relates weight to height with one number. It was designed to compare populations, and it is still the first check doctors use because it needs nothing but a tape measure and scales. For most adults a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is associated with the lowest health risks; higher and lower values are a prompt to look more closely, not a verdict.

## The formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

The healthy weight range for a height comes from rearranging the same formula: 18.5 × height² to 24.9 × height². At 175 cm that is 56.7 kg to 76.3 kg, or about 125 lb to 168 lb. BMI Prime is BMI ÷ 25. Categories are applied to the BMI rounded to one decimal, as published charts do, so 24.96 counts as overweight while 24.94 is healthy.
