  - q: How do I calculate a percentage of a number?
    a: "Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. 15% of 240 is 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = 36. The first calculator on this page does this and shows the working underneath."
  - q: How do I work out percentage change?
    a: "Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. From 80 to 100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%, an increase. A negative result is a decrease. A change from zero is undefined, because you cannot divide by zero."
  - q: What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?
    a: "Percentage change has a direction: it measures from a starting value to a new one, so 80 to 100 is +25% while 100 to 80 is −20%.   - q: What are percentage points?

## Percentages in one place

Most percentage questions are one of a handful of patterns: a share of a number, one number as a share of another, a change between two values, or an adjustment up or down by a rate. This page lays each pattern out as a sentence with the numbers in it, so you pick the one that reads like your question and fill in the blanks. Every answer comes with the working, which makes it easy to check or copy into a spreadsheet.

## The formulas

| Question | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| What is X% of Y | X ÷ 100 × Y |
| X is what % of Y | X ÷ Y × 100 |
| X is Y% of what | X ÷ (Y ÷ 100) |
| Change from A to B | (B − A) ÷ absolute value of A × 100 |
| Increase Y by X% | Y × (1 + X ÷ 100) |
| Decrease Y by X% | Y × (1 − X ÷ 100) |
| Difference between A and B | gap between A and B ÷ their average × 100 |

Percentage change divides by the absolute value of the starting number, so a move from −50 to −25 reads as a 50% increase, which matches how accountants describe a shrinking loss.
