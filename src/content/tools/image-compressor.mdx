## Why compress images

Images are usually the heaviest part of a web page and the first thing an email or upload form rejects. A photo straight from a phone is often 3 to 5 MB, while the same photo at quality 80 and a sensible size is 200 to 400 KB, small enough to load fast on a mobile connection and still look sharp. Compressing before you publish improves page speed, which search engines measure, and saves storage everywhere the file goes next.

This compressor works on a whole folder at once. Drop the images, set the quality or a size limit, and every file is processed in your browser with a running total of what you saved. The compare slider shows each result against its original, so you can lower the quality with confidence.

## Quality or target size

Quality is the simplest control: one number applied to every lossy image. Target size is for the moments when a limit is fixed, such as a job portal that accepts photos up to 200 KB. The tool tries a handful of qualities per image and keeps the highest one that fits, so a simple image keeps more detail than a busy one while both stay under the limit.

The format matters as much as the setting. WebP is typically 25 to 35 percent smaller than JPG at the same visual quality and keeps transparency, so switching to WebP is the biggest single saving for PNG screenshots and graphics.
