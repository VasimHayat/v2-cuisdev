To calculate a tip in your head, find 10 percent first by moving the decimal point one place to the left, then build the tip you want from it. Double it for 20 percent, add half of it for 15 percent, and for 18 percent take 20 percent and subtract a fifth of the 10 percent figure. On a $64.30 bill, 10 percent is about $6.40, so a 20 percent tip is about $12.80.

When you want the exact figure, a split between several people, or tip on the pre-tax amount, the [Tip Calculator](/tools/calculators/tip-calculator/) does it instantly. The rest of this guide shows the mental shortcuts step by step, so you can do it at the table.

## Why start with 10 percent?

Ten percent is the one percentage everybody can find without thinking. Moving the decimal point one place to the left divides by 10:

- $64.30 becomes $6.43
- $47.80 becomes $4.78
- $85.00 becomes $8.50

Every common tip is an easy step from there. You never have to multiply by 0.18 in your head.

## How do you calculate common tip percentages?

| Tip | Mental method | On a $64.30 bill (exact) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | Move the decimal one place left | $6.43 |
| 15% | 10%, plus half of that | $9.65 |
| 18% | 20%, minus 2% (a fifth of the 10% figure) | $11.57 |
| 20% | 10%, doubled | $12.86 |
| 22% | 20%, plus 2% | $14.15 |
| 25% | A quarter of the bill: halve it, then halve again | $16.08 |

The exact amounts in the table are rounded to the nearest cent.

### How do you calculate a 20 percent tip?

Find 10 percent and double it.

- Bill: $64.30
- 10 percent: $6.43
- Doubled: $12.86

If doubling $6.43 feels awkward, round first: $6.40 doubled is $12.80, only six cents away.

### How do you calculate a 15 percent tip?

Find 10 percent, then add half of it.

- Bill: $47.80
- 10 percent: $4.78
- Half of that: $2.39
- Total tip: $4.78 + $2.39 = $7.17

That matches the exact 15 percent of $47.80, which is $7.17.

### How do you calculate an 18 percent tip?

Work out 20 percent, then take off 2 percent. Two percent is a fifth of your 10 percent figure, or simply 1 percent doubled.

- Bill: $85.00
- 20 percent: $17.00
- 1 percent: $0.85, so 2 percent is $1.70
- Tip: $17.00 minus $1.70 = $15.30

### How do you calculate a 25 percent tip?

A quarter of the bill. Halve it, then halve it again.

- Bill: $64.30
- Half: $32.15
- Half again: about $16.08

## Should you round the bill first?

Rounding the bill to a friendly number first makes everything easier, and the error is tiny.

Take the $64.30 bill again. Round it to $64:

- 10 percent of $64 is $6.40
- 20 percent is $12.80, against an exact $12.86
- 15 percent is $6.40 + $3.20 = $9.60, against an exact $9.65

A few cents either way rarely matters. If you want to be sure you are not under-tipping, round the bill up rather than down: 20 percent of $65 is $13.00.

## How do you work out the total and split it?

Add the tip to the bill, then divide by the number of people.

- Bill: $64.30
- 20 percent tip: $12.86
- Total: $77.16
- Split three ways: $25.72 each

When the total does not divide evenly, someone has to pay one cent more. For example, $99.71 split two ways is $49.855 each, so one person pays $49.86 and the other $49.85. Rounding both shares up to $49.86 would collect a cent too much.

A simpler table habit is to round each person's share up to the next whole dollar, and treat the extra as part of the tip.

## Should you tip on the amount before or after tax?

Many people tip on the pre-tax amount, since the tax is not part of the service, while others simply tip on the total because it is easier. The difference is usually small. On a $58.00 meal with $5.22 in tax, a 20 percent tip is $11.60 on the pre-tax amount and $12.64 on the total.

Whichever you choose, the mental method is the same. Just start from the right number.

## What about less common percentages?

Most odd percentages are still one small step from 10 percent.

### 12.5 percent

12.5 percent is exactly one eighth. Halve the bill three times:

- Bill: $64.30
- Half: $32.15
- Quarter: about $16.08
- Eighth: about $8.04

The exact figure is $8.0375, which rounds to $8.04.

### 30 percent

Triple the 10 percent figure. On $64.30, 10 percent is $6.43, so 30 percent is $19.29.

### Any percentage

For any whole-number percentage, find 1 percent by moving the decimal point two places to the left, then multiply by the percentage. On $64.30, 1 percent is $0.643, so 7 percent is about $4.50. This is slower than the shortcuts above, but it works for anything.

## Is there a faster way with a phone calculator?

Yes. Instead of calculating the tip and then adding it, multiply the bill by one plus the tip rate to get the total in a single step:

| Tip | Multiply the bill by | $64.30 becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15% | 1.15 | $73.95 |
| 18% | 1.18 | $75.87 |
| 20% | 1.2 | $77.16 |

Then divide the total by the number of people if you are splitting. Two keystrokes give you everyone's share.

## How do you tip on a discounted or comped meal?

When part of the meal was discounted with a voucher or given free, many people tip on what the bill would have been before the discount, because the service was the same. Use the mental methods above on the original amount, then add that tip to what you actually pay. If you are unsure of the original total, the itemized receipt usually shows it.

## Why is percentage math so easy to get wrong?

Most mistakes come from multiplying by the wrong decimal. Multiplying by 1.5 instead of 0.15 gives a tip ten times too big, and dividing by 15 instead of multiplying by 0.15 gives nonsense. Starting from 10 percent avoids that, because you only ever move the decimal point, double, halve or subtract.

The same building-block approach works for discounts and price increases. Our guide to [calculating percentages](/blog/calculate-percentages/) covers those, and the [Percentage Calculator](/tools/calculators/percentage-calculator/) shows the working for each type of question.

## Quick reference

Keep these in mind and you can handle almost any bill:

- **10%:** move the decimal one place left.
- **5%:** half of 10 percent.
- **1%:** move the decimal two places left.
- **15%:** 10 percent plus 5 percent.
- **18%:** 20 percent minus 2 percent.
- **20%:** double 10 percent.
- **25%:** halve the bill twice.

Tipping customs differ a lot between countries and between kinds of service, from service included in the bill to no tipping at all, so check what is normal where you are. The math is the same everywhere.
